The map combines four indicators into a neighbourhood cooling-need score. Darker cells indicate higher relative need for intervention.

How is cooling need assessed?

The four inputs are population density, socioeconomic disadvantage (SEIFA IRSD), vegetation need (NDVI), and land surface temperature. Individual indicator maps show each input separately.

Why a 250 m grid?

Each 250 m × 250 m cell summarises neighbourhood-scale conditions consistently. Conditions may vary within a cell, so rooftop polygons are assessed separately.

These are relative priority scores, not direct temperature predictions.

About the prototype

About the Green Roof Explorer

For planners: first identify neighbourhoods with greater cooling need, then shortlist rooftop candidates and compare progressively broader implementation scenarios.

The neighbourhood need analysis uses 250 m × 250 m grid cells. Candidate roofs are shown at building scale. Population, SEIFA IRSD, NDVI and surface temperature inform the combined need score.

Who could use this?

Local government planners and other stakeholders can explore relative neighbourhood cooling need, shortlist candidate sites and examine illustrative implementation options. The tool does not grant approvals or determine final project feasibility.

Data interpretation

All need indicators are presented as relative scores. The 250 m grid summarises neighbourhood conditions; individual rooftop boundaries are a separate screening output. The exact source dates, classification validation and model sensitivity should be checked against the project documentation before decisions are made.

Responsible use

Roof screening may be incomplete or misclassified. Building structure, access, planning controls, ownership, drainage, waterproofing and costs require independent checks. Modelled cooling outcomes are illustrative and should not be treated as measured local changes.

Scenario outputs: cooling is illustrative and literature-derived, not a calibrated prediction of future local air temperature.
